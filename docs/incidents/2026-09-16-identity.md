# Incident: identity provider unreachable, 16 September 2026

**Duration:** 41 minutes · **Impact:** nobody could sign in. Existing sessions
kept working. No data was exposed and nothing was lost.

## What happened

The identity service stopped responding at 09:12. Anyone already signed in
carried on working — tokens are verified offline against a shared key, so the
service is only needed to *issue* one. Anyone arriving at the hub, or whose
hourly token expired, could not get in.

Two scheduled jobs failed at 09:30 because they request a service token at
start-up. Both were retried successfully after recovery.

## Why it was not worse

**Token verification does not call the identity service.** That is a direct
consequence of ADR-1 choosing a library over services: each app verifies
locally, so an identity outage degrades sign-in rather than halting every app.
Had we built the central auth service we considered, this would have been a
total platform outage instead of a 41-minute sign-in outage.

**The audit sink was unaffected**, so People Analytics stayed up. Had the
*audit* service been the one to fail, their app would have returned 503 by
design (ADR-2) — and that is the correct outcome, but worth remembering as the
one dependency that can stop a restricted-tier app outright.

## Root cause

A configuration change set the signing-key environment variable on the service
but not on its health check, which began failing and triggered a restart loop.
The service was never actually broken; the probe was.

## What we changed

- The health check now reads the same configuration path the service does, so
  it cannot disagree with it.
- The hub's status strip distinguishes "cannot sign in" from "apps are down" —
  during the incident it reported neither usefully, and people assumed the
  platform was gone when most of it was fine.

## What we deliberately did not change

**We did not add a fallback issuer.** A second way to mint tokens is a second
thing that can mint tokens wrongly, and sign-in being unavailable for 41 minutes
cost less than that risk. Revisit if this happens twice, or if an outage lands
during a month-end close.

**We did not shorten the retry window on jobs.** Two failed jobs that retried
cleanly is the system working.

## What this says about the design

The failure mode was shaped by a decision made months earlier: offline token
verification. It is the clearest evidence so far that ADR-1's trade — worse
patch distribution, no central runtime dependency — was the right way round for
a team this size.
